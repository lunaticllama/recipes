    Fresh Egg Pasta Dough

    Directions

    1. To make the dough: Mound flour in the center of a large, clean work surface, then use your fingers or the bottom of a bowl to push flour out from the center into a wide ring with a 4-inch well in the middle. Crack the whole eggs and egg yolks into the well, then add 1 teaspoon kosher salt.

    2. Use a fork to gently beat the eggs and salt together until smooth, keeping the liquid contained inside the flour well. Continue beating while slowly drawing small amounts of flour from the inner walls of the well into the eggs until a thick, paste-like batter forms and is no longer runny.

    3. Using a bench scraper or your hands, fold the remaining flour into the egg mixture until a shaggy, cohesive dough forms. Clean your hands and work surface of any dried dough bits so they do not get worked into the pasta.

    4. Knead the dough by pressing the heel of your hand into the ball, pushing forward and down, then rotating 45 degrees and repeating. Continue kneading until the dough is smooth, elastic, and has the firm consistency of Play-Doh, about 8 to 10 minutes. (If the dough feels too wet and sticky, knead in flour 1 teaspoon at a time; if too dry and crumbly, mist lightly with water or add a tiny amount of egg yolk.) Wrap tightly in plastic wrap and let rest on the counter for at least 30 minutes (or refrigerate up to 24 hours; bring back to room temperature before rolling).

    5. To roll the dough: Cut the rested dough into quarters. Set one piece on a lightly floured work surface and keep the remaining pieces covered with plastic wrap or a damp towel so they do not dry out. Flatten the piece with a rolling pin into an oblong shape about 1/2-inch thick.

    6. Set a pasta machine to its widest setting and pass the flattened dough through 3 times. Fold both short ends of the dough sheet toward the center so they meet, then fold in half like a book. Flatten slightly with a rolling pin and pass through the widest setting 3 more times.

    7. Narrow the pasta machine by one setting and pass the dough through, repeating the folding and rolling process once or twice until the sheet is smooth and even. Continue narrowing the settings one notch at a time, passing the dough through once or twice per setting without folding, until it reaches your desired thinness (usually the second- or third-thinnest setting for noodles, or the thinnest setting for stuffed pastas). Lightly dust with flour as needed to prevent sticking. Repeat with the remaining dough quarters.

    8. To cut and cook: Cut the rolled sheets to your desired length (about 12 to 14 inches) and run them through the pasta cutter attachment, or loosely roll up lightly floured sheets and slice by hand with a sharp knife. Toss cut noodles with flour, gather into loose nests on a parchment-lined baking sheet, and cover with a clean towel until ready to cook.

    9. Bring a large pot of generously salted water to a boil. Add the fresh pasta and cook until tender yet al dente, about 90 seconds to 2 minutes (a bit longer if dried or frozen). Drain and toss immediately with your sauce of choice.
